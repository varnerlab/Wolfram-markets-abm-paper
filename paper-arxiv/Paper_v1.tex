\begin{abstract}
A change in how traders respond to their neighbors can spread through
a market, but its price consequence depends on whether the resulting
orders execute. We developed a three-state cellular automaton market
to examine this connection with explicit trader holdings and dealer
capacity. Paired trajectories shared the same realized market state
until a fraction of interior traders changed one entry of their local
response rule. Persistent activation produced Sell majorities above a
window of activation fractions. A one-time Sell pulse at the same sites
produced no majority crossings at the tested fractions, while several
other single-entry rule changes did. Holding boundary traders inactive
preserved the transition, showing that local interactions could sustain
collective selling without price feedback. Larger lattices had a sharper
response on refined fraction grids, although the midpoint continued to
shift with the observation horizon. Dealer capacity changed majority
probability non-monotonically when prices fed back through boundary
traders. Greater capacity increased drawdown at the main activation
fraction across the tested price-impact coefficients. Cash and share
interventions traced the price consequence to order eligibility and
execution: trader holdings limited available flow, dealer resources
bounded unmatched fills, and executed imbalance changed the quote.
Similar Sell majorities could therefore accompany price declines that
differed by more than an order of magnitude. An unlimited dealer allowed all eligible selling to execute,
while finite capacity could limit the decline despite persistent
Sell-state dominance.
\end{abstract}
