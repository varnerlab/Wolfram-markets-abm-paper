\begin{abstract}
Can activating a new local behavior destabilize a market model that was stable
immediately before the change? We tested this question in a deterministic
cellular automaton whose agents held Buy, Hold, or Sell states. The baseline and
activated rules differed at one neighborhood sum. Each paired experiment ran
both paths under the baseline rule for 1,000 steps and then switched a
preselected fraction of sites in one path without resetting any dynamic state.
The matched path kept every site under the baseline rule. None of the matched
no-switch controls reached a Sell majority. With price feedback and an unlimited dealer, switching 10\% or
15\% of sites caused no transitions, whereas switching 20\% or 25\% caused all
50 runs to transition. Sell-majority transitions persisted when boundary
traders were held in the Hold state, showing that the local rule dynamics did
not require price feedback. Across lattice sizes 32--128, the activated
fraction associated with 50\% transition probability fell from 0.218 to 0.166,
and the transition width narrowed from 0.0275 to 0.0082. This finite-time
crossover sharpened with size but did not establish an equilibrium critical
point. Dealer capacity primarily controlled the price response. At 20\%
activation, median drawdown rose from 0.40\% with capacity of 0.1 unit per
trader to 3.92\% with one unit per trader and 25.3\% with unlimited capacity.
Separate cash and inventory interventions confirmed that trader eligibility
and dealer capacity constrained different stages between a desired order and
its effect on price.
\end{abstract}
