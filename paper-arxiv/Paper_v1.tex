\begin{abstract}
We study crash-like transitions in a deterministic, three-state cellular automaton on a
two-dimensional grid. Interior agents update from their four nearest neighbors using one
of two totalistic rules, and their mixture fraction~$f$ is the main control parameter.
In Model~A, QQQ minute data drive a subset of boundary cells. The model shows a sharp
finite-horizon crossover from a mixed state to a sell-dominated state. Sell domains form
locally and spread across the grid as coherent fronts. Mean-field and pair
approximations do not reproduce this spatial behavior. Across random initial conditions
and rule placements, onset times follow an inverse-distance empirical curve consistent
with a nucleation interpretation. Model~B replaces the external price with an endogenous
feedback loop and filters desired orders through finite cash and share inventories.
In the present experiments, budgets shift onset times less than twofold and
instead alter post-onset domain growth. A paired cash intervention
supports a price-to-boundary feedback mechanism, although the result is specific to the
model's execution and market-maker assumptions. The finite rule space makes the same
analysis possible for every totalistic rule in this model class.
\end{abstract}
