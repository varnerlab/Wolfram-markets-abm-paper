\begin{abstract}
How much local behavioral change is needed to produce a Sell-dominated
state, and how do execution constraints shape its price consequences? We
studied these questions in a deterministic three-state cellular automaton
market. After a common baseline history, a preselected fraction of interior
traders changed one entry of their local response table, while a matched
no-switch path continued from the same realized state. Persistent activation
produced a finite-time transition window that survived removal of price
feedback; a one-time Sell pulse at the same sites did not reproduce it.
Screening all single-entry neighbors of the baseline found additional
transition-producing rules, alongside changes that produced no majority
transition at the tested fractions. Adaptive fraction-grid refinement and
seed-bootstrap intervals quantified the size-dependent response, with
longer-horizon checks separating the reported finite-time crossover from
claims about asymptotic stability. Trader cash and inventory determined order
eligibility, an explicit dealer filled unmatched flow within its resource
limits, and executed imbalance moved the price. Dealer capacity changed
transition probability non-monotonically when prices fed back through boundary
traders, while strongly ordering drawdown at the main activation fraction.
These results distinguish local behavioral amplification, trader eligibility,
and dealer execution capacity as separate stages in this model. Their
separation allows the effects of the unlimited-counterparty assumption to be
measured within a simulation, without calibrating the model to real-market crashes.
\end{abstract}
