\begin{abstract}
Large market declines are not always traceable to external news of comparable size, which
raises the question of whether local interactions among traders can generate crash-like
transitions on their own. We studied that question in a deterministic, three-state
cellular automaton in which agents on a two-dimensional grid hold Buy, Hold, or Sell
states and update from their four nearest neighbors under Wolfram totalistic rules. The
interior mixed a majority rule with a contrarian rule that differs at a single
neighborhood sum, and the contrarian fraction served as the only control parameter. In
the exogenous-price model, boundary agents classified real QQQ minute-bar prices through
a volatility band and injected the result into the grid. Sweeping the contrarian fraction
produced a sharp finite-horizon crossover from a mixed state to a sell-dominated one:
sell domains nucleated locally and spread as coherent fronts that neither a mean-field
map nor a pair approximation reproduced, and onset times followed an inverse-distance
empirical curve consistent with a nucleation interpretation. The endogenous-price model
replaced the external feed with a price formed from the grid's own executed order
imbalance and gave every agent finite cash and share inventories, so sentiment had to
pass a budget before reaching the market. The crossover survived those budgets, onset
times were nearly indifferent to the cash endowment, and what the endowment governed
instead was how fast the winning domain grew after birth. A paired cash intervention
traced that gating to the one-cell-deep boundary ring through which price information
reenters the grid, a mechanism specific to the model's execution and market-maker
assumptions. Because the totalistic rule space is finite and enumerable, the same
analysis extends to every rule in this model class.
\end{abstract}
