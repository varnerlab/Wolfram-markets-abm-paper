\begin{abstract}
Can a local rule change destabilize a market that was stable immediately
before the change, and how does finite counterparty capacity alter the
outcome? We studied a deterministic cellular automaton in which agents held
Buy, Hold, or Sell states. Two local rules differed at one neighborhood sum.
In each paired rule quench, every site used the baseline rule for 1,000 steps.
A preselected fraction then switched rules without resetting the lattice,
price, adaptive boundary, trader books, or dealer. No dormant control reached
a Sell majority. With an endogenous price boundary and an unlimited dealer,
none of 50 runs transitioned at activated fractions 0.10 or 0.15, whereas all
runs transitioned at 0.20 or 0.25. The transition remained when the boundary
was held fixed. Across lattice sizes $L=32$--128, the held-boundary midpoint
moved from $f_{50}=0.218$ to 0.166, the transition width narrowed from 0.0275
to 0.0082, and peak ensemble susceptibility increased from 21.2 to 232.7.
Dealer capacity changed the financial expression much more strongly than the
spatial transition. At $f=0.20$, median drawdown increased from 0.40\% at
$h=0.1$ to 3.92\% at $h=1$ and 25.3\% at $h=\infty$. Independent cash and
share interventions further separated trader order eligibility from dealer
capacity. Time-sliced lattices documented Sell-domain growth across doses and
dealer conditions. We used those spatial and finite-size measurements without
claiming classical nucleation or an equilibrium universality class.
\end{abstract}
