\begin{abstract}
Whether activating a new local behavior can destabilize a market that was
stable immediately before the change is a causal question that separately
initialized parameter sweeps cannot answer. Execution poses a second gap,
because most interaction models compress the path from a desired trade to a
price change into a single stage. We addressed both in a deterministic
cellular automaton whose agents held Buy, Hold, or Sell states on a lattice
and updated from their nearest neighbors under a totalistic rule, with the
baseline and activated rules differing at exactly one neighborhood sum. Each
paired experiment evolved two identical paths under the baseline rule, then
switched a preselected fraction of interior sites in one path without
resetting any dynamic state, while the matched path continued unchanged. Agent
states generated unit orders, trader cash and inventory decided eligibility,
an explicit dealer absorbed unmatched flow up to a capacity set per trader and
rejected the rest, and executed imbalance moved the price, which re-entered
the lattice through traders on the perimeter. Holding those perimeter traders
inactive removed price feedback without changing the interior rules or the
financial bookkeeping. No matched control reached a Sell majority, whereas
switched paths converted the lattice above a narrow band of activated
fractions, and the transitions persisted once price feedback was removed,
which located the instability in the local rule dynamics rather than in
prices. Enlarging the lattice sharpened the crossover without establishing an
equilibrium critical point over the sampled sizes and horizon. Dealer capacity
instead governed how strongly the imbalance moved the price, and separate cash
and inventory interventions distinguished trader eligibility from dealer
capacity as constraints on different stages between a desired order and its
effect on price. An unlimited counterparty is therefore a consequential
modeling assumption rather than a neutral execution convention.
\end{abstract}
