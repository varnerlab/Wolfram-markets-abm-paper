\subsection{One complete simulation step}

Traders' current Buy, Hold, or Sell decisions generated orders before
the new quote could influence their next decisions
(Algorithm~\ref{alg:model-step}). We checked whether traders had the required cash or shares, matched
orders, and completed trades before calculating a new price
(Algorithm~\ref{alg:dealer-clearing}).
Orders at step $t$ came from the lattice $X_{t-1}$ and stored boundary
states $e_{i,t-1}$. Trades completed at the current price $P_{t-1}$ before the difference
between completed buys and sells generated $P_t$. The technical traders then
used this price to decide whether to buy, hold, or sell, and their
decisions influenced interior traders when calculating $X_t$.
Rule activation occurred before this sequence without changing the current
state. When we intentionally changed trader cash or shares, we did so before
checking whether orders had the resources needed to complete. This timing explains why a
rule switch leaves the first post-switch quote unchanged, whereas a
financial intervention can affect it immediately. The comparison with the technical traders kept in Hold used the same sequence
but prevented them from responding to price. Under either condition, a shortage of resources could stop an order
without changing the trader's cellular-automaton state. We recorded
orders excluded by trader resources as agent suppression and eligible
orders left unfilled by the dealer as dealer rejection. Neighboring
traders continued to respond to the stored Buy, Hold, or Sell state
even when the associated order did not execute.

\begin{algorithm}[!ht]
\caption{One market step with the technical traders active, $t-1\rightarrow t$, in simulation order.
Orders came from the old interior and boundary states, settled at the old
quote, and generated the new quote. The new quote set the boundary before the
interior cellular automaton update.}
\label{alg:model-step}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\REQUIRE lattice $X_{t-1}$; boundary states $e_i(t-1)$; trader books; dealer
ledger; quote and band state $(P_{t-1},\mu_{t-1},\sigma_{t-1})$; rule field
$R_i$; parameters; simulation seed
\ENSURE lattice $X_t$; boundary states $e_i(t)$; settled books and dealer;
quote and band state $(P_t,\mu_t,\sigma_t)$; all order counts
\IF{$t$ is the scheduled rule-switch step}
  \STATE $R_i\leftarrow19286$ for every preselected target site $i$
    \COMMENT{preserve every dynamic state variable}
\ENDIF
\IF{$t$ is a scheduled balance-sheet intervention}
  \FORALL{traders $i$}
    \STATE $w_i\leftarrow g_w w_i$; $q_i\leftarrow\operatorname{round}(g_q q_i)$
  \ENDFOR
\ENDIF
\STATE call Algorithm~\ref{alg:dealer-clearing} using states at $t-1$ and
$P_{t-1}$
\STATE receive $(n_t^B,n_t^S,u_t^B,u_t^S,r_t^B,r_t^S)$ and settled ledgers
\STATE $\phi_t\leftarrow(n_t^B-n_t^S)/N_{\rm tr}$
\STATE $P_t\leftarrow P_{t-1}\exp(\lambda\phi_t)$
\STATE $\mu_t\leftarrow\mu_{t-1}+\alpha(P_t-\mu_{t-1})$
\STATE $\sigma_t\leftarrow\sigma_{t-1}+\alpha(|P_t-\mu_t|-\sigma_{t-1})$
\IF{technical traders are active}
  \FORALL{technical traders $i$}
    \STATE $e_i(t)\leftarrow0$ if $P_t<\mu_t-k_i\sigma_t$; $2$ if
    $P_t>\mu_t+k_i\sigma_t$; $1$ otherwise
  \ENDFOR
\ELSE
  \STATE $e_i(t)\leftarrow1$ for every technical trader $i$
\ENDIF
\STATE write $e_i(t)$ to the technical traders' sites; leave the other edge sites at Hold
\FORALL{strictly interior sites $i$, simultaneously}
  \STATE $c_i(t)\leftarrow\sum_{j\in\partial i}s_j$ using old interior states
  and new boundary states
  \STATE $s_i(t)\leftarrow\mathcal{R}_{R_i}(c_i(t))$
\ENDFOR
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\begin{algorithm}[!ht]
\caption{Order eligibility, dealer clearing, rationing, and settlement at
$P_{t-1}$. The finite dealer filled only orders that other traders did not match. The
$h=\infty$ branch filled every eligible order.}
\label{alg:dealer-clearing}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\REQUIRE old interior and boundary states; books $(w_i,q_i,\bar c_i)$; dealer
$(W_{t-1},Q_{t-1})$ and bounds $(Q_{\min},Q_{\max},C)$; $P_{t-1}$; $h$;
cost-basis toggle; seed and step $t$
\ENSURE settled books and dealer; executed counts $(n_t^B,n_t^S)$; agent
suppression $(u_t^B,u_t^S)$; dealer rejection $(r_t^B,r_t^S)$
\STATE $\mathcal{B},\mathcal{S}\leftarrow\varnothing$; $u_t^B,u_t^S\leftarrow0$
\FORALL{interior traders, followed by technical traders $i$}
  \STATE $o_i\leftarrow +1$ for Buy, $-1$ for Sell, and $0$ for Hold
    \COMMENT{a technical trader reads its stored boundary state}
  \IF{$o_i=+1$ and $w_i\geq P_{t-1}$}
    \STATE append $i$ to $\mathcal{B}$
  \ELSIF{$o_i=-1$, $q_i\geq1$, and the cost-basis test passes}
    \STATE append $i$ to $\mathcal{S}$
  \ELSIF{$o_i=+1$}
    \STATE $u_t^B\leftarrow u_t^B+1$
  \ELSIF{$o_i=-1$}
    \STATE $u_t^S\leftarrow u_t^S+1$
  \ENDIF
\ENDFOR
\STATE $B_t\leftarrow|\mathcal{B}|$; $S_t\leftarrow|\mathcal{S}|$;
$n_t^B\leftarrow B_t$; $n_t^S\leftarrow S_t$
\IF{$h<\infty$ and $B_t>S_t$}
  \STATE $K_t^B\leftarrow Q_{t-1}-Q_{\min}$
    \COMMENT{shares that the dealer could sell}
  \STATE $n_t^B\leftarrow S_t+\min(B_t-S_t,K_t^B)$
\ELSIF{$h<\infty$ and $S_t>B_t$}
  \STATE $K_t^S\leftarrow Q_{\max}-Q_{t-1}$
    \COMMENT{inventory space}
  \STATE $K_t^S\leftarrow\min\!\left(K_t^S,
  \left\lfloor(W_{t-1}+C)/P_{t-1}\right\rfloor\right)$
    \COMMENT{cash limit; $C=0$ in this study}
  \STATE $n_t^S\leftarrow B_t+\min(S_t-B_t,K_t^S)$
\ENDIF
\STATE $r_t^B\leftarrow B_t-n_t^B$; $r_t^S\leftarrow S_t-n_t^S$
\IF{$r_t^B>0$}
  \STATE sort $\mathcal{B}$ by $z(\mathrm{seed},t,i,+1)$, then by trader identity
\ENDIF
\IF{$r_t^S>0$}
  \STATE sort $\mathcal{S}$ by $z(\mathrm{seed},t,i,-1)$, then by trader identity
\ENDIF
\FORALL{the first $n_t^B$ traders $i$ in $\mathcal{B}$}
  \STATE $\bar c_i\leftarrow(\bar c_iq_i+P_{t-1})/(q_i+1)$;
  $q_i\leftarrow q_i+1$; $w_i\leftarrow w_i-P_{t-1}$
\ENDFOR
\FORALL{the first $n_t^S$ traders $i$ in $\mathcal{S}$}
  \STATE $q_i\leftarrow q_i-1$; $w_i\leftarrow w_i+P_{t-1}$
    \COMMENT{$\bar c_i$ was unchanged on a sale}
\ENDFOR
\STATE $W_t\leftarrow W_{t-1}+P_{t-1}(n_t^B-n_t^S)$
\STATE $Q_t\leftarrow Q_{t-1}+n_t^S-n_t^B$
\RETURN $(n_t^B,n_t^S,u_t^B,u_t^S,r_t^B,r_t^S)$ and settled ledgers
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Rationing and paired reproducibility}

When buy and sell orders backed by trader resources differed, the
dealer limited the remaining trades if it lacked the resources to
complete them all.
Each eligible trader on that side received a 64-bit score from a
SplitMix64 finalizer applied to
$(\text{seed},t,\text{trader identity},\text{side})$.
Orders were sorted by score, using trader identity to break ties
reproducibly. The dependence on $t$ prevented a trader from retaining the
same priority across repeated orders. Paired paths used the same score
for a given $(t,i,\text{side})$ even after their eligible sets diverged.
Scoring did not consume draws from the generator used to initialize the
lattice and target sites. For each pair, we checked the frame at
$T_{\rm pre}$, its preceding Sell history, the technical traders' coordinates
and band multipliers, and the price history through the first post-switch
quote. For cash and share comparisons with the technical traders kept in Hold,
we also required the full Sell history and final grid to match exactly
across all nine changes to trader resources. Exact pre-switch agreement ensured that a pair reached activation
with the same history. Exact post-switch agreement across financial
interventions with the technical traders held ensured that trader resources affected execution
without entering the local rule update.

\subsection{Conservation and limiting cases}

Settlement conserved total cash and shares across traders and dealer
when no intervention occurred:
\begin{align}
 \sum_i w_i(t)+W_t &= \sum_i w_i(0)+W_0,\\
 \sum_i q_i(t)+Q_t &= \sum_i q_i(0)+Q_0.
\end{align}
The maximum absolute cash error across the production dealer-capacity and grid-size simulations was $2.53\times10^{-6}$; the share error was zero.
In the cash and share comparisons, checks were applied separately before
and after the intentional resource change. Its maximum cash error was
$9.47\times10^{-7}$, again with zero share error. Three limiting cases
also supplied regression checks. Unlimited capacity filled every eligible
order and reproduced the earlier fill-all implementation. Zero capacity allowed only trades between traders, giving equal
numbers of completed buys and sells and a fixed price. Interior
decisions were then identical whether the technical traders were active or
kept in Hold. Capacity $h=10$ never
rejected an order in the dealer-capacity simulations and matched the unlimited
path. That last equality applies to the stated endowments and horizon.
A different sequence of buy and sell orders, larger trader holdings,
or longer observation could cause this dealer to run out of cash or
reach its limit on shares.

\subsection{Simulation design and reproducibility}

We used four main sets of simulations to measure the effects of rule
adoption, dealer capacity, grid size, and trader resources, totaling 30,550 simulations
(Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:campaigns}). Each used 50 seeds and 3,000 post-activation
steps as fixed campaign settings. Pilot and full modes recorded how the experiments were developed.
The seed count and horizon were fixed campaign settings, without a
prospective power calculation or independent preregistration of the horizon. With 50 seeds, a probability near one-half has a binomial
standard error of about 0.07. The dealer-capacity simulations reused one no-switch
control within each $(h,\text{technical traders},\text{seed})$ cell. Its comma-separated value (CSV) file
therefore stored the control metrics with each treated summary row rather
than as a separate trajectory for every fraction. Checkpointed campaigns
reconstructed a fixed row order after threaded computation. A resumed campaign therefore retained the same control comparisons
and row order as an uninterrupted run. Reusing the no-switch path
avoided repeating an identical simulation for each activation fraction.

The selected paired-switch campaign used $L=64$, an unlimited dealer,
and active technical traders at $f=0.10,0.15,0.20,0.25$.
The dealer-capacity comparison used the same lattice size at fractions
0.10, 0.15, 0.175, 0.185, 0.190, 0.195, 0.200, 0.205, 0.210, 0.225,
and 0.250. It crossed these fractions with capacities
$h=0,0.1,0.5,1,2,5,10,\infty$ under both technical-trader conditions.
The original finite-size grid used fractions
0.140, 0.150, 0.160, 0.170, 0.175, 0.180, 0.185, 0.190, 0.200, 0.210,
0.225, and 0.250 at $L=32,48,64,96,128$.
Its three conditions were the technical traders held with $h=\infty$ and
active with $h=1$ or $\infty$. The cash--share campaign crossed nine
multiplier pairs with $f=0.170,0.175,0.180,0.185$ and
$h=0.1,1,\infty$ under both technical-trader conditions. Capacity was defined per trader
throughout; a fixed-absolute-capacity size series was not run.
The supplementary controls below retain this initialization while varying
the stated intervention, parameter, or observation window.

Reproducing a simulated market required the same random-number seed
for the starting conditions and the same order of calculations. We ran the simulations in Julia 1.12 with the
environment pinned by the repository manifest. Original campaigns used 12 simulation threads and one
garbage-collection thread; supplementary campaigns used four or eight
simulation threads and one garbage-collection thread.
A seeded Xoshiro generator~\cite{blackman2021scrambled} supplied the setup
draws. Finite-size and balance-sheet campaigns wrote atomic checkpoints,
then aggregated them in a deterministic order. Tests covered parameter
validation, dealer limits, conservation, delayed activation, technical-trader
controls, and time-varying priorities. Stepwise comparisons checked the
optimized lattice kernel against the general totalistic solver under both
homogeneous and heterogeneous rule fields. Bootstrap analysis and generated
LaTeX tables used Python, with package versions recorded in
\texttt{code/revision/requirements.txt}. Source code, environment manifests,
derived data, and figure scripts are available at
\url{https://github.com/varnerlab/Wolfram-markets-abm-paper}.
The repository also retains the earlier external-price classroom
implementation and its archived Invesco QQQ exchange-traded fund input
for provenance. No result from that model entered the principal analysis
or figures.

Majority probability measured how often at least half the interior
traders decided to sell, and onset measured how long that took. We used these summaries to compare
activation fractions, lattice sizes, and trader resources. In the selected switch campaign, transition status was
evaluated from peak Sell fraction because the stored onset used zero
for a path with no crossing. Median onset was therefore undefined at
the two lower fractions, where no switched seed transitioned
(Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:paired-switch}). The refined size comparisons used
midpoints, widths, and susceptibility maxima calculated over each
sampled fraction grid (Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:finite-size-scaling}). The
susceptibility peak location remained a sampled fraction rather than
an interpolated optimum (Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:revision-fits}). In the
financial campaign, all 27 conditions with the technical traders held returned
one majority probability at each fraction. The 27 conditions with them active
spanned a range of probabilities (Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:balance-fractions}).
When the technical traders stayed in Hold, majority probability depended on
neighbors' decisions at the given fraction of rule adoption. Allowing
the technical traders to respond to price added the influence of price changes
on later decisions. Each probability counted
crossings within its stated observation window, and each interpolated
midpoint depended on the fractions sampled around that crossing.

To check whether the displayed markets changed beyond the original
observation period, we extended the five displayed examples and all 50
original $f=0.150$ starting markets to 10,000 steps after the rule change
(Fig.~\ref{fig:dose-panels}; Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:market-panel-horizons}). The displayed
$f=0.150$ market belonged to that set, giving 54 distinct conditions, each
with a matched run that retained the original rule. These 108 simulations
used the same $64\times64$ grid, 1,000 initial steps, trader resources,
unlimited dealer, and active technical traders as their original
runs. They extended existing observations rather than providing independent
replications, and the examples were not reselected using the longer results.
We measured first passage to a Sell majority and maximum drawdown at every
step, then summarized outcomes at 3,000, 5,000, and 10,000 steps. Final Sell
fractions averaged the last 500 steps of each window. Archived trajectories
retained every tenth step, while the plotted histories retained every
step (Fig.~\ref{fig:dose-panels}). All 54 paired conditions reproduced their archived 3,000-step summary
measurements. Separate comparisons for the five displayed markets reproduced
the full original price and Sell-fraction histories and the final grid.
Checks also verified identical histories before the rule change and
conservation of cash and shares throughout the extended simulations.
