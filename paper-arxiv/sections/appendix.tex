\section{Mean-Field and Pair-Approximation Derivations}
\label{app:pair}
% ============================================================================

This appendix records the mean-field and pair-approximation calculations in the form in
which they were implemented. The quenched
rule mixture enters in its annealed form: Rules~19280 and~19286 share every entry of
their totalistic tables except at neighborhood sum $c = 1$, where the majority rule
outputs Buy and the contrarian rule outputs Sell, so a site whose rule assignment is
drawn afresh at minority fraction~$f$ follows the effective stochastic rule
\begin{equation}
  R_f(c \to b) \;=\; (1-f)\,\mathbf{1}\!\left[\mathcal{R}_{19280}(c) = b\right]
  \;+\; f\,\mathbf{1}\!\left[\mathcal{R}_{19286}(c) = b\right],
\end{equation}
deterministic at every $c \neq 1$ and splitting $c = 1$ between Buy, with
probability $1 - f$, and Sell, with probability~$f$.

The mean-field closure is that same effective rule with all spatial structure discarded:
each agent's four neighbors are drawn independently from the global state distribution
$(p_0, p_1, p_2)$, where $p_s$ is the fraction of agents in state~$s$ and
$p_0 + p_1 + p_2 = 1$. The neighborhood sum $c = s_N + s_S + s_E + s_W$ is then a
multinomial convolution of four i.i.d.\ draws,
\begin{equation}
  \pi_k \;=\; \Pr(c = k) \;=\; \!\!\sum_{\substack{n_0+n_1+n_2=4 \\ n_1+2n_2=k}}\!\!
  \binom{4}{n_0,\,n_1,\,n_2}\,p_0^{n_0}\,p_1^{n_1}\,p_2^{n_2},
  \qquad k = 0,1,\ldots,8,
  \label{eq:pi-dist}
\end{equation}
and the verified rule tables (Table~\ref{tab:rule-taxonomy}) send $c \in \{2,3\}$ to Buy,
$c \in \{4,5\}$ to Hold, and $c \in \{0,6,7,8\}$ to Sell for both rules, leaving $c = 1$
as the only entry on which they differ. The map is therefore
\begin{align}
  p_0' &= (1-f)\,\pi_1 + \pi_2 + \pi_3, \label{eq:mf-p0} \\
  p_1' &= \pi_4 + \pi_5, \label{eq:mf-p1} \\
  p_2' &= \pi_0 + f\,\pi_1 + \pi_6 + \pi_7 + \pi_8, \label{eq:mf-p2}
\end{align}
with primes denoting next-step fractions and each $\pi_k$ evaluated from
Equation~\eqref{eq:pi-dist} at the current $(p_0, p_1, p_2)$. We iterated
Equations~\eqref{eq:mf-p0}--\eqref{eq:mf-p2} to convergence,
$\lVert\mathbf{p}' - \mathbf{p}\rVert_1 < 10^{-14}$, from multiple initial conditions and
across $f \in [0, 1]$. The two stable fixed points reported in the main text are those of
this map, and the Jacobian at the buy-dominated branch has complex eigenvalues of modulus
$|\lambda| \approx 0.95$, a stable spiral. The pair approximation restores one level of
spatial structure.

The state of the pair approximation is the symmetric matrix of nearest-neighbor pair
densities $\rho_{ab}$, $a, b \in \{0, 1, 2\}$, normalized by
$\sum_{ab} \rho_{ab} = 1$, with site marginals $\rho_a = \sum_b \rho_{ab}$ and
conditional neighbor distribution $q(s \mid a) = \rho_{as} / \rho_a$. The closure is
the local-structure ansatz of Gutowitz, Victor, and
Knight~\cite{gutowitz1987local}: given a focal site in state~$a$, its three
neighbors \emph{other} than the paired partner are drawn independently from
$q(\cdot \mid a)$. Their sum~$m$ therefore follows the trinomial convolution
\begin{equation}
  \Pr(m = k \mid a) \;=\;
  \sum_{\substack{n_0 + n_1 + n_2 = 3 \\ n_1 + 2n_2 = k}}
  \binom{3}{n_0,\,n_1,\,n_2}\,
  q(0 \mid a)^{n_0}\, q(1 \mid a)^{n_1}\, q(2 \mid a)^{n_2},
  \qquad k = 0, \ldots, 6,
\end{equation}
and the focal site's full neighborhood sum is the partner's state plus that draw,
$c = a' + m$, which never exceeds~$8$. Averaging the effective rule over the
trinomial weights gives the transition kernel
\begin{equation}
  T_b(a, a') \;=\; \sum_{k=0}^{6} \Pr(m = k \mid a)\; R_f(a' + k \to b),
\end{equation}
the probability that a site in state~$a$ with paired neighbor in state~$a'$ lands in
state~$b$ after one synchronous step. Substituting this kernel for both members of a
pair (each site conditioning on its own partner) yields the synchronous pair update
$\rho'_{bb'} = \sum_{a,a'} \rho_{aa'}\, T_b(a, a')\, T_{b'}(a', a)$ quoted as
Equation~\eqref{eq:pair-update} of the main text. The implementation symmetrizes and
renormalizes $\rho'$ after every step to guard against numerical drift.

We iterated the map to its fixed points, declaring convergence when the largest
componentwise change $\max_{ab} |\rho'_{ab} - \rho_{ab}|$ fell below $10^{-12}$, and
every trajectory reported here converged within a few hundred iterations. Stability
of the uncorrelated fixed point $\rho^*_{ab} = \rho^*_a \rho^*_b$, the product state
built from the mean-field marginals, was assessed
through the Jacobian of the map restricted to the six independent components
$(\rho_{00}, \rho_{01}, \rho_{02}, \rho_{11}, \rho_{12}, \rho_{22})$ that survive
symmetry and normalization, computed by finite differences with step
$\delta = 10^{-7}$, each perturbed matrix projected back onto the probability simplex
before the map was applied. The spectral radius sat strictly below unity at every
value of~$f$ we sampled (twelve values from $0$ to $0.5$), declining monotonically
from $0.95$ at $f = 0$ to $0.71$ at $f = 0.5$ (Figure~\ref{fig:theory}B).

The search for correlated fixed points used initial conditions
\begin{equation}
  \rho_{ab} \;=\; p_a p_b + \varepsilon \left( \delta_{ab}\, p_a - p_a p_b \right),
\end{equation}
which interpolate between the uncorrelated product state at $\varepsilon = 0$ and
perfect same-state correlation at $\varepsilon = 1$. We ran
$\varepsilon \in \{0.1,\, 0.3,\, 0.5\}$ on buy-biased marginals
$(0.50,\, 0.30,\, 0.20)$, $\varepsilon \in \{0.1,\, 0.3\}$ on sell-biased marginals
$(0.20,\, 0.10,\, 0.70)$, and $\varepsilon = 0.5$ on $(0.10,\, 0.05,\, 0.85)$,
together with an uncorrelated uniform control, at thirteen
values of~$f$ between $0$ and $0.5$. Every trajectory converged either to the
uncorrelated buy-dominated fixed point or to the all-Sell absorbing state, with the
spatial mutual information between adjacent sites decaying to zero in all cases. That
mutual information is $I = 2S_1 - S_2$, built from the single-site and pair Shannon
entropies
\begin{equation}
  S_1 \;=\; -\sum_{a} \rho_a \ln \rho_a,
  \qquad
  S_2 \;=\; -\sum_{a,b} \rho_{ab} \ln \rho_{ab},
  \label{eq:entropies}
\end{equation}
in which $S_1$ is the site entropy plotted for the lattice in the main text
(Figure~\ref{fig:theory}A), maximal at $\ln 3$ for a uniform mixture of the three states
and zero in a single-state configuration. Because the two site marginals of a symmetric
$\rho_{ab}$ are equal, $I$ vanishes exactly when the pair densities factorize as
$\rho_{ab} = \rho_a \rho_b$. The pair approximation therefore supports no correlated
fixed point, which is the negative result on which the nucleation argument rests.

\section{Model~B Stage~2 Factorial}
\label{app:stage2}
% ============================================================================

The Stage~2 factorial sweep crossed six factors in full, giving the design from which
every Model~B summary statistic in this study is drawn
(Table~\ref{tab:stage2-design}).

$5 \times 4 \times 3 \times 2 \times 3 \times 3 = 1{,}080$ configurations, each run
at $10$ seeds for $2{,}000$ steps ($10{,}800$ runs).}
\label{tab:stage2-design}
\small
\begin{tabular}{llc}
\toprule
Factor & Levels & Count \\
\midrule
Minority fraction $f$   & $0,\ 0.1,\ 0.15,\ 0.2,\ 0.3$  & 5 \\
Price impact $\lambda$  & $0.005,\ 0.01,\ 0.02,\ 0.05$  & 4 \\
Initial cash            & $500,\ 1{,}000,\ 5{,}000$     & 3 \\
Disposition rule        & off, on                       & 2 \\

Band half-width $k$     & $0.5,\ 1,\ 2$                 & 3 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

Every run was assigned to one of five candidate regimes by a classifier evaluated in
strict order, each test applying only to runs that fail all earlier ones. A run with
zero return standard deviation is \emph{inert}. Failing that, a run is
\emph{crash-prone} when its crash frequency, the fraction of steps at which the
grid's Sell fraction stands at or above $0.5$, reaches at least $0.01$. Failing
that, a run is \emph{oscillatory} when the net order imbalance $\varphi$ changes sign
at least $20$ times per $2{,}000$ steps. That threshold is a rate, one flip per $100$
steps, stated here against the sweep's horizon so that it transfers to other run
lengths. Failing that, a run whose total price range stays below $0.10\,P_0$ is a
\emph{plateau}, and any remaining run is a \emph{drift}. Of the $10{,}800$ runs,
$6{,}589$ ($61\%$) were oscillatory and $4{,}211$ ($39\%$) crash-prone; no run was
ever inert, plateau, or drift. The sell-dominated (crash-prone) regime appeared
abruptly between $f = 0.15$ and $f = 0.20$ at every price impact $\lambda$ in the
design.

Grouping runs by their parameter configuration split each summary metric's variance into
a between-configuration share, attributable to the swept parameters jointly, and a
within-configuration share, attributable to the disorder seed. The parameters accounted
for $94$--$97\%$ of the variance across the four summary metrics, at between-configuration
shares of $0.946$ for crash frequency, $0.952$ for return kurtosis, $0.959$ for
return standard deviation, and $0.974$ for the $\varphi$ sign-flip count, leaving
the seed at most $5.4\%$. Across matched design cells, enabling the disposition rule increased
return standard deviation by a factor of $2.40$ on average.

The crash-prone class divided into the modal freeze and a collapsing tail, the
decomposition on which the transition analysis builds: the median crash-prone run
had a maximum drawdown of about $5\%$ and a price minimum of $100.00$, so half the
class never traded below the initial quote at all, while $897$ of the $4{,}211$ runs
drove the price below $90$ and $451$ below $80$. The deepest collapses, drawdowns of
$40$--$69\%$ arriving after bubbles of $2.5$--$2.7\times$ the initial price, were
concentrated at $f = 0.2$, $\lambda = 0.05$, cash $5{,}000$, disposition off, the
loosest budget regime in the design.

\section{Escape-Time Censoring and Survival Detail}
\label{app:censoring}
% ============================================================================

The escape-time campaign ran each seed to a
horizon of $2{,}000$ steps, and any run that has not nucleated by then is
right-censored: its onset time $\tau = t_{\mathrm{on}}$ is treated as exceeding the
horizon, and the run enters the Kaplan--Meier estimator carrying that censoring
mark, since censored runs are never dropped. The resulting counts are recorded for every
$(f, \text{cash})$ cell of the campaign (Table~\ref{tab:censoring-counts}); rows at $20$
seeds belong to the pilot pass and rows at $40$ seeds to the refinement pass. The
companion velocity--suppression dose-response added
two further cells at $f = 0.20$, cash $250$ and cash $2{,}000$, each with $20$ seeds,
one censored run, and an onset rate of $0.95$. Those cells feed the dose-response
analysis, not the barrier fits.

Because censoring occurs only at the horizon, the Kaplan--Meier survival curve of a
cell steps down at each observed onset and its median is finite exactly when the
onset rate reaches one half. The fits therefore rest
on five points at cash $500$, at $f \in \{0.177,\, 0.18,\, 0.20,\, 0.25,\, 0.30\}$, and
on six points at cash $1{,}000$ and $5{,}000$, which add $f = 0.174$
(Table~\ref{tab:kramers-fits}). Cash $500$ has
one fewer because its onset rate at $f = 0.174$ was $0.45$, just below the one-half a
finite median requires. It was this gap that prompted extending the refinement grid
to $f = 0.177$, so that the tightest budget still contributed a point hard against
the cliff. The reported confidence intervals (Table~\ref{tab:kramers-fits},
Figure~\ref{fig:s4}) come from $2{,}000$ bootstrap resamples drawn over seeds
within each~$f$: each resample recomputes the Kaplan--Meier medians and refits the
barrier law, and the intervals are the $2.5$th and $97.5$th percentiles of the
resampled parameters.

Finally, the onset-rate observation near the cliff is visible
directly in Table~\ref{tab:censoring-counts}, and it carries the same label as in the
main text: exploratory, reported uncorrected for multiple comparisons, and
weighed accordingly. At $f = 0.174$ the onset rate rose monotonically in cash,
$0.45$, $0.62$, $0.70$; at $f = 0.177$ the Kaplan--Meier medians fell in the same
order, $1{,}598$, $1{,}427$, and $1{,}208$ steps.
