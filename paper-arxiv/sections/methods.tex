\section{Methods}
% ============================================================================

\subsection{Three-state cellular automaton}

Agents occupied an $L\times L$ square lattice and carried one of three states:
Buy ($s=0$), Hold ($s=1$), or Sell ($s=2$). We refer to the synchronous local
update as a cellular automaton (CA). Only the $(L-2)^2$ strictly interior cells
followed the CA rule. For interior cell $i$, we calculated the sum of its four
cardinal-neighbor states as:
\begin{equation}
  c_i(t)=\sum_{j\in\partial i}s_j(t)\in\{0,1,\ldots,8\}.
  \label{eq:neighborhood-sum}
\end{equation}
A totalistic rule maps each possible sum to one of the three states. Writing
the nine outputs as ternary digits $a_j={\cal R}(j)$ gave the Wolfram rule
index:
\begin{equation}
  R=\sum_{j=0}^{8}a_j3^j.
  \label{eq:rule-index}
\end{equation}
The numerical state labels therefore entered the dynamics through both the
neighborhood sum and the rule table.

We used Rule~19280, $(222110002)_3$, as the baseline and Rule~19286,
$(222110022)_3$, as the perturbed rule. The tables differed only at
$c=1$. A neighborhood with three Buy cells and one Hold cell therefore
returned Buy under Rule~19280 and Sell under Rule~19286. We used the term
\emph{contrarian} only for that specific local response. For each run, we
selected a fraction $f$ of interior sites uniformly without replacement and
kept that target set fixed. All target sites initially used Rule~19280 and
changed to Rule~19286 only at the intervention step. The initial interior
contained exactly 5\% Buy and 5\% Sell sites placed without replacement, with
Hold elsewhere. Randomness entered through that initial placement, the target
set, the active boundary sample, boundary band widths, and finite-dealer
rationing. Conditional on the seed, the complete dynamics were deterministic.

\subsection{Orders, trader books, and dealer clearing}

Every interior cell and every active boundary cell represented a trader. A
Buy state submitted one unit buy order, a Sell state submitted one unit sell
order, and a Hold state submitted no order. Trader $i$ carried cash $w_i$,
integer inventory $q_i$, and average acquisition cost $\bar c_i$. A buy was
eligible when $w_i\geq P_{t-1}$, and a sell was eligible when $q_i\geq1$. We
disabled the optional cost-basis restriction in the principal experiments, so
average cost did not prevent a sale. We recorded an order that failed these
tests as \emph{agent-suppressed}. Suppression changed the financial execution,
not the trader's CA state.

We let $B_t$ and $S_t$ denote the eligible buy and sell counts at the standing
quote $P_{t-1}$. Opposing flow of size $\min(B_t,S_t)$ cleared without using
dealer capacity. The residual went to an explicit dealer. Dealer capacity was
an intensive quantity $h\geq0$ in net units per trader. For $N_{\rm tr}$
traders, we set $H=\operatorname{round}(hN_{\rm tr})$. A finite dealer began
with cash $W_0=P_0H$ and inventory $Q_0=H$, could not borrow, and satisfied
$0\leq Q_t\leq2H$. When buys exceeded sells, the executed counts were:
\begin{align}
 n_t^S &= S_t, &
 n_t^B &= S_t+\min(B_t-S_t,Q_{t-1}).
 \label{eq:dealer-buy-excess}
\end{align}
When sells exceeded buys, the corresponding counts were:
\begin{align}
 n_t^B &= B_t, &
 n_t^S &= B_t+\min\!\left(S_t-B_t,2H-Q_{t-1},
                 \left\lfloor\frac{W_{t-1}}{P_{t-1}}\right\rfloor\right).
 \label{eq:dealer-sell-excess}
\end{align}
We recorded eligible units beyond these bounds as \emph{dealer-rejected}.

When the dealer rejected part of the excess side, a counter-based score keyed
by seed, step, trader identity, and side supplied a new reproducible priority
ordering. The time key prevented the same trader from retaining priority at
every step. Filled orders updated trader books at $P_{t-1}$. The dealer
received the opposite cash and share transfers, which gave its ledger update:
\begin{align}
  Q_t &= Q_{t-1}+n_t^S-n_t^B,\
  W_t &= W_{t-1}+P_{t-1}(n_t^B-n_t^S).
  \label{eq:dealer-ledger}
\end{align}
Total cash and shares over traders plus dealer were conserved when no
balance-sheet intervention occurred. The $h=\infty$ limit started from a zero
dealer ledger, permitted borrowing and shorting, and filled every eligible
order. It was the unlimited limit of the same clearing rule, not a separate
execution model.

\subsection{Price feedback and the held-boundary control}

Only executed orders moved the price. We calculated normalized executed
imbalance as:
\begin{equation}
  \phi_t=\frac{n_t^B-n_t^S}{N_{\rm tr}}.
  \label{eq:imbalance}
\end{equation}
The imbalance updated the standing quote according to:
\begin{equation}
  P_t=P_{t-1}\exp(\lambda\phi_t).
  \label{eq:price-update}
\end{equation}
The principal experiments used $P_0=100$ and $\lambda=0.02$. Active boundary
traders classified the new quote against an exponential moving center and an
exponential moving absolute deviation. We updated those quantities as:
\begin{align}
 \mu_t &= \mu_{t-1}+\alpha(P_t-\mu_{t-1}),\
 \sigma_t &= \sigma_{t-1}+\alpha(|P_t-\mu_t|-\sigma_{t-1}),
 \label{eq:adaptive-band}
\end{align}
where $\alpha=1/60$. Boundary trader $j$ had a fixed half-width
$k_j\sim U[k(1-\delta),k(1+\delta)]$, with $k=1$ and $\delta=0.5$. It took Buy
below $\mu_t-k_j\sigma_t$, Sell above $\mu_t+k_j\sigma_t$, and Hold inside the
band. One quarter of perimeter cells were active, and the rest stayed at Hold.

The simulation wrote the new boundary state before it updated the interior.
The complete feedback sequence was:
\begin{equation*}
 \text{CA state}\rightarrow\text{orders}\rightarrow\text{dealer fills}
 \rightarrow P_t\rightarrow\text{boundary state}\rightarrow\text{CA state}.
\end{equation*}
The implementation applied rule activation, any balance-sheet intervention,
eligibility tests, clearing, settlement, price formation, boundary
classification, and the synchronous CA update in that order
(Algorithms~\ref{alg:model-step} and~\ref{alg:dealer-clearing}). The matched
\emph{held-boundary} control
retained the same initial lattice, target rule field, active edge coordinates,
band draws, books, dealer, orders, and price calculation, but replaced every
active boundary state with Hold before the interior update. Financial state
continued to evolve in this arm, but it could not return to the lattice. The
held boundary therefore isolated the autonomous spatial response.

\subsection{Paired rule-quench experiments}

Every principal experiment began with Rule~19280 at every interior site. The
system evolved for $T_{\rm pre}=1{,}000$ steps before any rule changed. At the
top of step $T_{\rm pre}+1$, the treated arm activated Rule~19286 at the
preselected fraction $f$; the dormant arm left the same target sites on
Rule~19280. Neither arm reset the lattice, quote, adaptive bands, trader books,
or dealer. The two paths were identical through $X_{T_{\rm pre}}$. The next
quote was also identical because it came from that common state, and the first
possible difference occurred in the following interior update. We verified
these equalities bit for bit for every pair.

The dealer-capacity screen used $L=64$, 3,000 post-activation steps, and 50
seeds. The fraction grid was 0.10, 0.15, 0.175, 0.185, 0.190, 0.195, 0.200,
0.205, 0.210, 0.225, and 0.250. Dealer capacity took values 0, 0.1, 0.5, 1,
2, 5, 10, and $\infty$ under endogenous and held boundaries. Traders began
with cash 1,000 and 10 shares. The finite-size experiment used
$L\in\{32,48,64,96,128\}$ and three arms: held boundary with $h=\infty$,
endogenous boundary with $h=\infty$, and endogenous boundary with $h=1$. It
sampled 12 fractions from 0.140 to 0.250 over 50 seeds. Because $h$ was defined
per trader, absolute dealer capacity scaled with lattice size.

The balance-sheet experiment changed trader funding and sell inventory at the
same top-of-step event as rule activation. We multiplied current cash and
current shares independently by $g_w,g_q\in\{0.1,1,10\}$ and rounded shares to
integers. The full $3\times3$ design was crossed with
$f\in\{0.170,0.175,0.180,0.185\}$, $h\in\{0.1,1,\infty\}$, both boundary
conditions, and 50 seeds. All nine interventions within a
$(f,h,\text{boundary},\text{seed})$ cell shared an identical pre-intervention
path. Under the held boundary, their full lattice paths also had to remain
identical after the intervention. That equality supplied an exact null test
for a direct effect of financial variables on the interior rule.

\subsection{Spatial panels, outcomes, and reproducibility}

We chose the time-sliced market panels from ensemble summaries before
inspecting their lattice images. The dose atlas included a nontransitioning
$f=0.15$ run, both outcome groups at $f=0.175$, and transitioning runs at
$f=0.185$ and 0.20 under $h=\infty$. Within each transitioning group, we chose
the onset nearest the subgroup median. Within each nontransitioning group, we
chose the final Sell fraction nearest the subgroup median. The dealer atlas
required a seed that transitioned at $h=0.1$, 1, and $\infty$ for $f=0.20$.
Among those seeds, we minimized the summed distance from the three
capacity-specific median onsets. Both atlases displayed the lattice at fixed
offsets 0, 500, 1,500, and 3,000, so rows shared one clock.

The order parameter $m(t)$ was the strictly interior Sell fraction. We called
a run transitioned when $m(t)\geq0.5$ at least once after activation and
defined onset as the first crossing. We reported transition probability,
onset conditional on crossing, the final 500-step mean of $m(t)$, maximum
post-activation drawdown, agent-suppression fractions, and the fraction of
eligible orders rejected by the dealer. At fixed $(L,f,h)$, we measured
between-seed fluctuations of the final-window mean $\bar m$ with the ensemble
susceptibility:
\begin{equation}
 \chi_L=(L-2)^2\left[\langle\bar m^2\rangle-
                         \langle\bar m\rangle^2\right].
 \label{eq:susceptibility}
\end{equation}
We linearly interpolated the sampled transition curve to estimate the
finite-horizon midpoint $f_{50}(L)$ and the width $f_{75}(L)-f_{25}(L)$.
These quantities described the sampled crossover and were not treated as
equilibrium critical parameters.

Binomial proportions used 95\% Wilson intervals, and continuous summaries
used interquartile ranges unless noted otherwise. A seeded Xoshiro generator
produced the setup draws. Each production cell wrote an atomic checkpoint,
and aggregation reconstructed a fixed row order, so thread scheduling and
restart order did not alter the results. Unit tests covered parameter
validation, finite-dealer limits, time-varying reproducible priorities,
conservation, delayed rule activation, the held-boundary control, and exact
reproduction of the earlier fill-all path at $h=\infty$. Step-by-step tests
also compared the optimized CA kernel with the general totalistic solver for
homogeneous and heterogeneous rule fields.
