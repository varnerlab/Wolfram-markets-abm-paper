\section{Conclusion}
% ============================================================================

Switching one local response destabilized market-model paths that remained
stable under the baseline rule. The matched no-switch paths established this
causal effect within the model, while the held boundary and finite-size
experiments showed that the spatial transition did not depend on price
feedback. Dealer capacity instead determined how strongly the resulting order
imbalance changed the price or produced rejected orders. Trader cash, trader
inventory, and dealer resources constrained different stages of execution.
Sell-state dominance, price drawdown, and dealer exhaustion can therefore move
separately, and an unlimited counterparty should be treated as a consequential
modeling assumption rather than a neutral execution convention.
