\section{Conclusion}

Changes in investor decision rules destabilized the modeled market when
enough traders received a new response to their neighbors. A Sell majority
emerged while otherwise identical markets retained mixed decisions under the
original rule. Asking the same traders to sell once did not produce these
majorities, while several other changes to a single response did. The
fraction of traders that needed the new rule depended on grid size and
observation time. Keeping traders at the edge in Hold showed that decisions
based on neighbors alone could sustain widespread selling without
information about price changes. Completing those sales required shares,
buyers with cash, and dealer resources for orders that other traders could
not match. When edge traders responded to price, greater dealer capacity
increased drawdown in the comparison where every capacity produced a Sell majority even though a smaller
dealer could make a majority more likely at some lower fractions. Market
instability and a price crash were therefore separate outcomes of investor
decision-making in this model. Exhausted dealer capacity limited the price
decline even while many traders continued deciding to sell.
