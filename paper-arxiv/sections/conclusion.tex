\section{Conclusion}

A lasting change in one local trading response produced Sell majorities
from paired market paths that remained mixed under the baseline rule.
The matched one-time Sell pulse did not reproduce those transitions,
while several other single-entry rule changes did. The activation
fraction needed for a majority depended on lattice size and observation
time. Holding boundary traders inactive showed that local interactions
could sustain the spatial transition without price feedback, so whether
selling spread was settled on the lattice. Its financial consequence was
settled at execution: trader cash and shares determined eligibility,
and dealer cash and inventory limited the unmatched flow that could
fill. Restoring price feedback also allowed these constraints to change
majority probability non-monotonically, and greater dealer capacity
increased drawdown at the main activation fraction even though a
smaller dealer could produce a higher majority probability near the
activation window. Within this model, collective selling and a large
price decline were therefore separate outcomes. Many traders could
remain in Sell after the dealer exhausted its ability to buy their
shares.
