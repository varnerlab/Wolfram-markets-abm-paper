\section{Conclusion}
% ============================================================================

We studied a deterministic three-state cellular automaton with two totalistic rules on
a two-dimensional lattice, and in the exogenous-price model a fixed-seed sweep showed a sharp
finite-horizon crossover between $f=0.14$ and $f=0.15$, above which localized sell
domains grew through coherent fronts that neither mean-field nor the tested pair closure
reproduced. Onset times were well summarized by an inverse-distance empirical curve, a
fit consistent with a nucleation interpretation but neither a derived Kramers law nor
evidence of an infinite thermodynamic barrier, and the Potts energy~\cite{potts1952some}
similarly measures spatial order without generating the CA dynamics.
The endogenous-price model added price feedback and finite cash and share inventories. Birth
sites were consistent with uniform interior placement, and the present experiments
bounded any cash-associated difference in onset time to within a factor of two, whereas
budgets were associated with post-onset growth. In paired simulations, a tenfold cash
injection just before the control's detected onset accelerated the front in $14$ of
$19$ included pairs by changing the price-mediated boundary state. This result is
specific to the model's unmodeled infinite-inventory market maker and uniform cash-shock
intervention.
The finite set of $3^9=19{,}683$ totalistic rules makes systematic extension possible.
Future work should recheck the budget results at other grid sizes,
compare alternative onset-time models under censoring, include explicit counterparties,
and test whether the same spatial mechanism appears for other rule pairs.
