\section{Conclusion}

Persistent local rule activation produced a finite-time Sell-majority
transition from realized baseline paths. The matched pulse control did not
reproduce the effect, and a single-entry rule screen showed both additional
transition-producing changes and changes with no majority response at the
tested fractions. Refined fraction grids, bootstrap intervals, and horizon
checks delimit this conclusion; they do not establish an equilibrium critical
point or asymptotic stability of the baseline.

The second question concerns transmission to price. A held boundary showed
that the spatial transition can occur without price feedback, while the
endogenous boundary allowed dealer capacity to alter transition probability
non-monotonically. Trader resources determine eligibility, dealer resources
limit execution of unmatched flow, and the executed imbalance determines the
quote. These distinct stages permit similar Sell-state dominance to accompany
very different drawdowns. In this model, an unlimited counterparty is a
consequential execution assumption whose effects can be measured separately
from the local behavioral instability.
