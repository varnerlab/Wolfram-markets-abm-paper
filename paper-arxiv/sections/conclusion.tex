\section{Conclusion}

A lasting change in the way a minority of traders formed their
intentions could turn a mixed market into one dominated by sellers, even
when no trader's decision responded to the price. The fraction of
traders needed depended on the number of interacting traders and on how
long the market was observed, and active technical traders let financial
conditions change later decisions. A Sell majority did not by itself
determine the price decline. Trader cash and shares limited which orders
could be completed, and the dealer's reserve curve set the prices at
which net trades could clear. Shallow positive reserves accompanied
severe declines, whereas zero dealer depth allowed only trades between
traders at a fixed price. Limited resources therefore did not protect
the market from a simulated crash in general. The financial consequence
of widespread selling depended on how resources entered execution and
price formation, not only on how many traders decided to sell.
