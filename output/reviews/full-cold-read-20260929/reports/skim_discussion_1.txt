discussion.P1: A lasting single-response rule change assigned to a fraction of interior traders produced Sell majorities that a one-time Sell instruction did not. The result depends on which response was changed. Paired no-switch controls isolate the switch effect, and rules were assigned, not learned.
discussion.P2: Selling spread even with no price-responsive decisions, through seller groups that grew and merged. The fraction needed for half the markets to reach a majority moved with observation time, so no infinite-size or long-time threshold and no nucleation are claimed.
discussion.P3: The size of the price decline came from the constant-product dealer. Shallow positive reserves allowed large declines with few rejected orders, zero depth is a fixed-price special case, and paired controls were needed because shallow-reserve markets crashed without the switch too.
discussion.P4: Finances affected decisions only through the price-reading technical traders, and holding them is an exact control. Dealer depth lowered drawdown but did not consistently change majority probability, robustness checks on band adaptation and depth were inconclusive, and majority and drawdown are separate outcomes. The paragraph is overloaded.
discussion.P5: Compared with learning and strategy-choice ABMs, this model gives up learning in exchange for an exactly repeatable assigned rule switch, price feedback that can be switched off, and separately measured intentions and prices, so it answers narrower questions.
discussion.P6: Lists limitations: assigned, synchronous, sum-based rules; intentions visible without resources; one band rule and edge placement; an uncalibrated constant-product dealer with fixed fees; no order book, dividends or borrowing. The closing conservation and stationarity caveats (S9-S11) cannot be followed as written.
