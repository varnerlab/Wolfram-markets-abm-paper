results.P1: A lasting one-entry rule change (Buy to Sell at neighbor sum 1) given to at least 20% of interior traders produced a Sell majority in every market, while 15% or less produced none and no control produced one. A one-time Sell pulse to the same traders never did. Of the 18 one-entry changes to Rule 19280, six always produced a majority and twelve never did.
results.P2: With technical traders held, majorities still formed and were identical across dealer depths, so responses to neighbors alone suffice. Price feedback shifted individual markets both ways inside a narrow crossover, and its net effect on majority probability was small and depended on the fractions averaged.
results.P3: Larger grids moved the rise in majority probability to smaller fractions and made it about three times narrower. The susceptibility far exceeds the bound for independent traders, so Sell activity is correlated. Longer observation lowers the midpoint about as much as doubling L, so the crossover depends on the observation period.
results.P4: Extending examples and the whole f=0.15 ensemble to 10,000 steps showed that some markets reach a majority late. The switch already raised drawdowns before any majority formed, and a late majority raised drawdown further.
results.P5: At f=0.20 every market reached a majority, but dealer depth set how far the price fell. Shallow reserves gave near-total declines with very few rejections, and no-switch controls also fell hard at shallow depth. The paired switch effect peaked at intermediate depth (h=5), and zero depth gave a fixed price.
results.P6: Scaling trader cash and shares leaves decisions unchanged when technical traders are held. With technical traders active it moves the majority probability (0.54-0.80) and strongly changes drawdown, especially through the share multiplier, while rejections stay small. A majority alone therefore does not determine the price fall.
