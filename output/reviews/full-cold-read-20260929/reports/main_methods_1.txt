methods.P1: The market is an L x L grid. Interior traders carry Buy/Hold/Sell codes 0/1/2 and all update at once from the sum of their four neighbors' codes. Price-reading technical traders on some edge sites update first each step, and the other edge sites stay in Hold.
methods.P2: A totalistic rule is a nine-entry table indexed by neighbor sum (3^9 rules, base-3 Wolfram numbering). Baseline Rule 19280 and switched Rule 19286 differ only at sum 1 (Buy vs Sell). A fraction f of interior traders is drawn in advance and switched at a scheduled time that is not stated. Runs start from a 5% Buy / 5% Sell seed, and the switch changes no holdings.
methods.P3: Each intention becomes a one-share order. Buys need cash for the price plus fee, and sells need one share. There is no loss-aversion constraint. Orders that fail for lack of cash or shares are "trader suppression". Intentions still influence neighbors even when they can't be carried out.
methods.P4: The dealer is a constant-product market maker starting with h shares per trader at P_0 = 100. Each step fixes the net direction at the no-trade price, then picks the largest net dealer trade that the one-share floor and the budget-eligible excess orders at that trade's own price allow. The smaller side fills completely and the other is rationed by a reproducible score. Eligible orders left unfilled are "dealer rejection", and phi_t = x_t / N_tr.
methods.P5: The dealer's reserves update after each step. The quoted price is W/Q, which moves with the squared share ratio and differs from the execution price. Fees go to a separate account. Cash and shares are conserved apart from scheduled interventions, and the constant product means a round trip cannot drain the dealer. h = 100 is a large-reserve comparison, and h = 0 is a fixed-price case with trades only between traders.
