discussion.P1: A lasting one-response switch (Rule 19280 to Rule 19286) given to a minority of traders produced Sell majorities that a one-time sell instruction did not. Outcomes depended on which response changed, paired controls isolated the switch's effect, and traders did not learn or pass on rules.
discussion.P2: Selling spread without any decision responding to price, through seller clusters growing and merging. The crossover's location moved with grid size and observation time, so no threshold for large or long-lived markets and no nucleation mechanism is claimed.
discussion.P3: The constant-product reserves set both execution and quoted prices. Shallow positive reserves allowed severe declines while rejecting few orders, zero depth is a fixed-price special case, and no-switch controls were needed because shallow markets fell sharply even without the switch.
discussion.P4: UNCLEAR - It opens with "financial conditions affect decisions only through technical traders (held = control)" and then drifts into non-monotonic depth dependence, an inconclusive band-freeze test with an unstated hypothesis, the depth confound, mechanism dependence, and the separability of majority and drawdown, with no unifying thread.
discussion.P5: Contrasts the model with learning-based ABMs (minority game, SFI, chartist-fundamentalist, lattice/CA markets). Here rules are assigned, price feedback is confined to edge traders, and resources constrain trades, which gives up realism for exact, repeatable interventions and narrower questions.
discussion.P6: Limitations: assigned, synchronous, totalistic rules; intentions visible without resources; one band rule and placement; an uncalibrated constant-product dealer with no strategy or order book; conservation is not realism; stationarity is unproven. Suggests extensions.
