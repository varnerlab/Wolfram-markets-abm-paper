introduction.P1: Real markets arise from interacting investors whose selling can spread and, when buyers are scarce, push prices down; it asks when a change in some investors' behavior causes widespread selling and when that selling becomes a crash.
introduction.P2: Reviews agent-based market models along two lines: price-mediated interaction (Farmer-Joshi market maker, Santa Fe market, the authors' double-auction market) and lattice herding models (Ising-type, Bornholdt, Iori); it ends by separating who influences whom (the arrangement) from how each trader responds (the rule).
introduction.P3: Cites 1-D and later CA market models, then describes this model: a 2-D lattice of interior traders using two rules that differ in one response, price-reading technical traders on the edge, Buy/Sell as intentions separate from budget-limited completed trades, and a finite constant-product dealer whose reserves set the quoted price that feeds back through the technical traders.
introduction.P4: States the aims and lists the experiments: paired rule-switch runs that vary the switched fraction to find a Sell majority, sweeps of size and duration, runs with technical traders held in Hold, separate sweeps of dealer reserves and trader holdings, and following/copying experiments on crowding and gains.
