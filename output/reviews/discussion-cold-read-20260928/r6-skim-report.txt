discussion.P1: A persistent, assigned change in some traders' responses can spread selling intentions without spreading the rule itself. The effect depends on the particular response changed, and paired controls identify its effect under the tested starting conditions and observation periods.

discussion.P2: Neighbor interactions can produce Sell majorities without price feedback. The observed spatial growth and sharper probability curves support finite-time spreading, but do not establish an asymptotic threshold or classical nucleation.

discussion.P3: The dealer's execution and pricing rule determines what limited resources do to prices. Small positive reserves can permit steep declines; a dealer with no reserves is a distinct fixed-price case. Paired controls distinguish the switch's contribution from declines that happen anyway.

discussion.P4: Financial changes affect interior intentions through price-responsive technical traders, while majority formation and drawdown can vary differently. The comparisons do not cleanly distinguish funding from price sensitivity, and the role of threshold adaptation remains unresolved, although the reason for the latter limitation takes reconstruction.

discussion.P5: The contribution is a controlled, repeatable test of assigned local rule changes and price feedback. It trades the learning and strategic behavior found in other models for a narrower, enumerable rule space and separately measured intention and price outcomes.

discussion.P6: The conclusions depend on assigned synchronous rules, observable intentions despite exhausted resources, particular technical traders and a specific dealer mechanism. The model is not calibrated to real crashes, accounting checks do not establish realism, and the finite simulations do not establish stationarity or indefinite behavior.
