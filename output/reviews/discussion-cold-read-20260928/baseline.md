Purpose: interpret the findings, distinguish spreading intentions from financial outcomes, position the contribution among existing models, and bound conclusions by model assumptions and evidence.

## P1: Study interpretation, persistent switch and paired attribution
First: In this study, we explored how a lasting change in the way a minority of traders formed their intentions could spread selling through a market, and what then determined how far the price fell.
Last: These conclusions concern assigned rules and their spread through neighbors: traders did not learn the new rule, adopt it for its profitability, or pass the rule itself to other traders.

## P2: Neighbor-driven spread, finite size/time and limits of physical analogies
First: The spread of selling did not require any decision to respond to the price.
Last: Thus, the supported mechanism was the spread of changed responses through neighboring decisions on finite grids over finite observation periods.

## P3: Dealer reserve mechanism and baseline-adjusted financial outcomes
First: How far the price fell depended on how the dealer's resources entered the price.
Last: Thus, the size of a crash depended on the execution and price mechanism as well as on the resources available, and scarce resources did not by themselves limit the decline.

## P4: Price feedback and unresolved mechanism of depth effects
First: Financial conditions could still change later decisions, but only through the technical traders, who responded to the quoted price.
Last: Neither one determined the other.

## P5: Comparison with established agent-based markets and scope of contribution
First: The model differed from established agent-based markets in where traders interacted and in what was changed.
Last: The questions answered were correspondingly narrower: not whether technical trading emerges or whether the market becomes efficient, but how many traders had to change a rule before a Sell majority formed, and whether its formation required feedback from price.

## P6: Model limitations and future tests
First: Several modeling choices limit how far these conclusions extend.
Last: Other decision rules, asynchronous updates, alternative interaction networks, and different execution mechanisms would test how the connection between local decisions and price declines depends on these assumptions.
