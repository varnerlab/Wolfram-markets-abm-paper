P1: Assigning a lasting change to one response in selected traders' rules can spread selling intentions to a majority, even while other traders retain their rules. Its persistence and the particular response changed matter. Paired controls isolate the intervention's effect at a shared starting state. This is a test of assigned behavior, not learning or adoption of successful strategies.

P2: Local responses to neighbors can produce a Sell majority without decisions responding to prices. Seller groups can grow and join, and majority formation depends on both the switched fraction and observation time. These finite observations support a local spreading mechanism but do not establish an infinite-system threshold or classical nucleation.

P3: Financial resources influence crash severity through the execution and pricing rule. A constant-product dealer with small positive reserves can accommodate selling at sharply falling prices. Removing dealer depth entirely creates a distinct fixed-price matching case. Large declines also occur without the intervention, so paired comparisons are needed to identify its additional effect.
